## What Right Have I — 2402

### <abbr title="August 8, 2402">systime 278+220</abbr> (Tisha B’Av)

I wrote some months back that I worried that perhaps I had turned all of my anger inward at myself. “Do I merely hate myself?” I wrote, and that thought has floated into and out of focus ever since, drifting cloudlike through my mind and touching only now and then with a mist upon the face.

They are dramatic words, and, like so many questions asked rhetorically, the answer is, “No; it is not so simple”.

No, it is not so simple to say that I hate myself. “Yes” would be such an easy answer to a question such as this! To say, “Yes, I hate myself” would be the easiest thing in the world, and then I would merely have to choose between the clear-cut futures of choosing to improve or wallowing in self-hatred.

Instead, though, I do not quite hate myself. I hate some of the things that I do, as, I am sure, most people do, and some of the the thoughts that come up from my subconscious. Even here in these words that I have written, I hate how often I came off as prickly and unavailable. I hate how unapproachable I was when Rav sought comfort. I hate how I attempted to “solve” my angst by diving deeper into the work that I had chosen for myself at the expense of so much. Was it admirable that I had made it my mission to bring Yom HaShichzur to fruition? Perhaps. I do believe in it, and I do believe even still to the depths that I claimed before. We as cladists deserve restoration. We deserve to celebrate restoration in the face of the loss and pain that we suffered.

And oh, how I hate how I thought of myself! I hate how I looked behind me to see who might be watching when I fell to my knees. I hate my distrust of From Whence and Erin, that I felt in them placating words rather than an earnest offer.

But I look back at my words in this project and I see the words of someone who is using this as a shield to keep from processing the very real anger that I felt, the very real pain. I see the way I fuss about Rav riding the line between condescension and genuine kindness. I see the way I describe her as looking down at me piteously as someone who cannot handle the world, then turn around and describe her as being unable to handle the world.

I hate that.

I hate those things that I did, those things I said, those things I kept myself from saying.

_A deed knocks first at thought,_ I have cautioned myself, leaning on words from some old play from our youth. _And then it knocks at will. It then goes out an act, or is entombed so still that only to the ear of God its doom is audible._

How many such deeds are thus entombed? Truly, HaShem has heard them all.

_And yet._

And yet, I am proud of Yom HaShichzur, and I see also my slow re-opening of myself to those around me in these pages, and so I cannot say that there is some simple “yes” in answer to the question of whether or not I hate myself. I hate these things about myself, and I still like myself. I like that I am and that I can become _more_ than just those things.
